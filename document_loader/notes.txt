Article 370 was a provision of the Indian Constitution that granted special status and significant autonomy to the former state of Jammu and Kashmir. It allowed the state to have its own constitution, a separate flag, and freedom to make its own laws on all matters except defense, foreign affairs, and communications.Key Details and HistoryOrigin: Included in the Constitution in 1950, Article 370 was drafted as a temporary provision to manage the transition of the former princely state into the Indian Union.Article 35A: Closely tied to Article 370, this 1954 provision allowed the Jammu and Kashmir state legislature to define "permanent residents" and grant them exclusive rights to property, state government jobs, and scholarships.Revocation of Special StatusOn August 5, 2019, the Government of India issued a Presidential Order that effectively revoked Article 370 and rendered Article 35A inoperative.Equal Application: All provisions of the Indian Constitution were extended to Jammu and Kashmir.
